# Xlets Website : Art Direction Brief

**Do not make this look like a generic AI-generated landing page. Treat this
like a real creative direction project with art direction, hierarchy, rhythm,
spacing, visual narrative, and a unique concept.**

Build the showcase for five Linux Mint Cinnamon desklets and applets
(World Clock, Color Timer Clock, Workspace Grid, In Panel Workspace Name,
Panel Profiles) as an award-worthy open-source product site that sits
alongside curbsoftware.com and buttons.curbsoftware.com without imitating
either.

The finished site should feel:

* art-directed
* editorial
* precise
* quietly alive
* premium
* bespoke
* gallery-like
* tactile
* minimal-but-not-empty
* intentional
* memorable

This is not a SaaS funnel. No pricing, no testimonials, no fake counts, no
logo strips, no newsletter capture, no invented statistics.

The one reaction to design for:

> This is beautifully made. These extensions look genuinely useful. I want
> to install them.

---

# 1. CORE CREATIVE CONCEPT

One idea carries the whole site:

## The desk, running.

These products ARE the desktop: workspace tiles, panel strips, clocks,
color schedules, saved layouts. So the site is a Cinnamon desktop mid-use.
Every visual device comes from the products themselves:

* workspace raster (the faint desktop grid)
* panel strip (the Cinnamon top bar becomes the site nav)
* desklet cards (the card language: sharp, hairline-bordered, floating on
  the desktop)
* live time (world clocks tick, the color card ramps)
* tile outlines (the active-workspace grammar: hover and focus outline
  tiles in mint)

Where curbsoftware is a highway at night and buttons is an editor at work,
this site is a desktop being used: tiled, precise, quietly alive.

Do not decorate with unrelated abstract spheres, glass blobs, random 3D
ribbons, stock illustrations, or generic dashboard chrome. Derive
everything from the xlets.

# 2. COLOR SYSTEM

Pinescale + mint. This is the anti-amber sibling: cool, green-tinted
graphite surfaces with a single mint accent. Amber appears exactly once on
the whole site: inside the Color Timer Clock schedule demo, as literal
product data (a schedule stop color). That scarcity makes it read as truth,
not brand.

* Neutrals: green-tinted graphite (OKLCH hue around 168, near-zero chroma).
  Dark theme background near 0.145 lightness; panels and raised surfaces
  step up in 0.03 to 0.05 increments. Light theme: warm paper whites.
* Accent mint: dark theme primary oklch(0.84 0.17 152); light theme
  oklch(0.62 0.15 155). Mint is precious: active states, key rules, the
  accent word in the hero, tile outlines. Never wash whole sections in it.
* Per-xlet accent hues tint exactly three things on that xlet's surface:
  the index numeral, the eyebrow rule, and the hover glow. World Clock
  240 (sky), Color Timer Clock 75 (schedule amber), Workspace Grid 152
  (mint), In Panel Workspace Name 300 (violet), Panel Profiles 25 (rose).
  One hue per page. Never mixed.
* Light AND dark themes ship. Time-of-day is literally what these products
  are about; day and night mode is the concept, not a chore. Dark follows
  system by default.

Keep `--radius: 0` everywhere. Square corners, hairline rules, hard-edged
controls. The family signature.

# 3. TYPOGRAPHY

Three fonts, all self-hosted variable, no external requests:

* Display: Bricolage Grotesque at wide widths, 600 to 700. Oversized
  editorial statements. Its slight quirk is the anti-generic signature.
* Body and UI: Hanken Grotesk. Warm, highly legible.
* Mono: Spline Sans Mono. Eyebrows, metadata, UUIDs, versions, install
  commands, clock and timezone labels, index numerals.

Three typographic modes:

* Editorial display: huge, tight leading (0.86 to 0.94), slightly negative
  tracking, confident weight, narrow measure. Hero around
  clamp(4.5rem, 8.5vw, 9rem); section statements 48 to 80 px.
* Interface: restrained, often mono, small uppercase metadata, tight
  spacing. The desklet-card voice.
* Reading copy: 55 to 70 characters per line, comfortable line height.
  Body 15 to 18 px. Never tiny 9 to 11 px text for atmosphere.

Use typography as layout: a section heading may occupy half the screen with
no card and no image around it.

# 4. HOME PAGE COMPOSITION

One continuous designed composition, not stacked sections. Vary density on
purpose: huge negative space here, layered UI there, an almost empty
editorial statement next, then a full-bleed screenshot. Avoid the
heading-paragraph-small-graphic repeating rhythm.

Sections, in order:

## 4.1 Hero, about 96svh, 12-column split

Left 5 columns: mono eyebrow "LINUX MINT CINNAMON · DESKLETS AND APPLETS";
headline broken intentionally:

    Your desktop,

    arranged.

with "arranged." in mint; a short lede in the house voice; primary CTA
"Install the set" to /install/; secondary "Browse the five" anchor.

Right 7 columns: a live micro-desktop built in HTML and CSS from the
products' own grammar. From back to front:

* a faint workspace raster backdrop
* a top panel strip carrying workspace name chips (the nav motif in
  miniature)
* a world-clock mini grid showing REAL times via Intl.DateTimeFormat for
  Vancouver, London, Tokyo (static build-time fallback under JS-off)
* one color-timer card whose background actually ramps between schedule
  stops
* a workspace grid with the active tile outlined in mint

Pointer parallax at depths 1, 3, 6, 10 px equivalents. Barely conscious.
The micro-desktop is the single wow element; keep it crisp enough to read.

## 4.2 Ticker

Immediately after the hero, a full-width mono strip, very slow marquee,
paused under reduced motion:

    VANCOUVER 09:41 · LONDON 17:41 · TOKYO 01:41 · UTC 16:41

Mint separators. It reads as the world clock breathing.

## 4.3 Xlet index

Five full-width rows, never a card grid. Per row: oversized index numeral
in that xlet's hue; name in display type; a DESKLET or APPLET mono chip;
tagline; version and license meta in mono; a cropped screenshot sliver at
the right edge that widens on hover. Hovering outlines the row like the
products outline the active workspace tile: the products' own grammar.

## 4.4 Why these exist

Editorial measure, personal copy from the README origin stories (the
second panel lost at boot, the color schedule as signal). One pull-quote
scale moment. No invented claims.

## 4.5 Install overview

A short version of the flow with a link to /install/. Mono, factual,
calm.

## 4.6 Spec strip

Product-spec line, not social proof:

    OPEN SOURCE · GPL-2.0+ · 3 DESKLETS · 2 APPLETS · LINUX MINT CINNAMON · GITHUB

## 4.7 Final CTA

A genuine closing scene: large near-full-viewport statement, the
micro-desktop's simplest form behind it (a tile, a clock, a panel strip in
outline), primary action to /install/, secondary to GitHub. Small mono
line: GPL-2.0+ · Open source · CurbSoftware.

# 5. DETAIL PAGE TEMPLATE

Shared layout so the five pages feel like one set, differentiated by
content, story, and one accent hue.

1. Hero: eyebrow (kind, version, license); huge name; tagline; meta rail
   (version, license, kind, repo, spices when live, derived-from credit);
   the hero screenshot framed like product photography: hairline frame,
   mint edge light catching the top, graphite shadow, slight 1 to 2 degree
   perspective, 60 to 70 vw. Never a small boxed image in empty space.
2. Why this exists: the README origin story at editorial measure. Keep the
   personal voice. One pull-quote scale moment.
3. Feature story: 2 or 3 alternating compositions. Features typeset as a
   mono-numbered editorial index beside the config screenshot, not
   icon-plus-paragraph cards.
4. Settings showcase: a re-created Cinnamon settings window fragment in
   HTML (section headers, checkbox rows, combobox rows, dependency
   labels) rendered from the real settings-schema labels. Truthful labels
   only; this is the tactility moment.
5. Gallery: full desk shot and config shot, large, edge-to-edge.
6. Install: mono command block with working copy button; tabs for release
   zip and git; commands derived from uuid and kind so all five pages
   agree.
7. Prev and next xlet strip with the set's index numerals; back to index.

Special case: In Panel Workspace Name screenshots are extreme panel strips
(487x85 master, 3840x2160 webp of a full panel). Treat the panel strip as
a full-bleed horizontal band across the page, not a boxed image. Let it
run edge to edge.

# 6. INSTALL PAGE

A quiet technical page. The general flow as numbered mono steps (01
download the zip, 02 extract to ~/.local/share/cinnamon/..., 03 reload or
re-enable, settings survive upgrades). Then a per-xlet command index, one
row per xlet, each with its working copy button. Facts only. Every command
verified against the monorepo READMEs.

# 7. NAV AND FOOTER

Nav: the Cinnamon panel strip is the nav. Left: wordmark "xlets" with a
small tile glyph plus a mono chip CURBSOFTWARE. Right: Xlets (index
anchor), Install, GitHub, theme toggle. Translucent blur surface after 24
px of scroll, like a real panel autohiding into focus. No rounded glass
pill.

Footer: a quiet technical appendix. Strong horizontal rule. Left: project,
GPL-2.0+, by CurbSoftware. Right: the five repo links, the monorepo,
curbsoftware.com, buttons sibling. Mono microtype. Do not overdesign.

# 8. MOTION DIRECTION

Cinematic, slow, restrained, purposeful. Vanilla IntersectionObserver and
CSS only; no libraries; no hydration.

* Reveal: elements enter with a 12 px rise and opacity over 500 to 700 ms,
  a 60 to 90 ms stagger between siblings.
* Hero pointer parallax: four depth planes, 1 to 10 px, rAF-throttled,
  disabled on touch and reduced motion.
* Ticker: slow CSS marquee, 60s+ per loop.
* Line-draw: section eyebrow rules draw from left when entering view.
* Live clocks: minute-precision updates; the color card ramps on a real
  interpolation, one transition per stop.
* Everything visible with JS blocked. Everything instant under
  prefers-reduced-motion: reduce. Motion is enhancement, never content.

# 9. MATERIAL AND DEPTH

Depth from realistic material behavior, not decoration: hairline borders
one luminance step up, one soft graphite shadow, a faint mint reflection
on framed screenshots, a site-wide grain at near-invisible opacity, tonal
background shifts between chapters (deep graphite hero, slightly raised
index, inverted-paper editorial moment if the rhythm needs it, deep
finale). Screenshots stay truthful: frame them, light them, never repaint
them.

# 10. RESPONSIVE ART DIRECTION

Recompose, do not shrink. Mobile hero: headline at strong but sane scale,
micro-desktop below the copy with 2 or 3 elements only, others hidden.
Index rows become full-bleed stacked bands with the numeral as a large
background figure. Detail hero screenshots go full width, perspective off.
Install steps stack vertically. No microscopic text or diagrams anywhere.

# 11. ACCESSIBILITY

WCAG AA minimum, 7:1 target for body text in both themes. Real anchors,
strong focus states (the mint tile outline IS the focus ring), semantic
headings, alt text on every screenshot, keyboard paths through tabs and
copy buttons, 200% zoom usable, no hover-only information, full
prefers-reduced-motion behavior.

# 12. QUALITY BAR

Before declaring any section done, ask:

> Could this section belong unchanged on 100 other developer-tool
> websites?

If yes, recompose it. Each section needs its own reason for looking the
way it does, derived from these products: tiles, panels, clocks, schedules,
saved layouts. The visual system should be impossible to separate from the
product concept.

Aim for the polish of a 10000 to 20000 dollar agency microsite with the
honesty, speed, and restraint of an open-source developer tool.

# 13. DO NOT

* no feature-card grids, bento layouts, or repeated bordered boxes
* no generic AI-landing look (centered hero, gradient blob, three cards)
* no rounded 16 to 32 px containers (square corners are the brand)
* no em-dashes or emojis anywhere, in code, comments, or copy
* no promo voice ("supercharge", "game-changing", "unlock")
* no fake metrics, testimonials, or logo clouds
* no external font or script requests
* no framework JS, no animation libraries
* no repainting product screenshots into something the xlets are not
* no Mint logos or Mint trademark trade dress; "Linux Mint" nominative
  only
